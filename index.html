What exists — and where it breaks
Existing tools solve 60–70% of the problem. The remaining gap is where your idea lives.
AI literature review tools (Consensus, Elicit, Scite)
Search and summarize papers well. But they don't connect literature to a specific patient population, trial design, or drug mechanism. Static, not agentic.
Gap → Can't reason across internal study data + external literature simultaneously. No longitudinal reasoning.
Pharmacovigilance platforms (Oracle Argus, Veeva Vault)
Structured case management but purely reactive. Rule-based signal detection. No contextual reasoning over unstructured narrative text in case reports.
Gap → Can't detect emerging signals across multi-modal sources (social, EHRs, literature) in real-time.
Clinical trial management (Medidata, Veeva CTMS)
Good at tracking protocol compliance after the fact. Site selection is still largely relationship-driven and retrospective.
Gap → Can't predict protocol deviations before they occur. Recruitment matching is keyword-based, not phenotypic.
Medical chatbots (Babylon, Ada Health)
Symptom checkers with decision trees. Not grounded in a patient's actual longitudinal record. No multi-turn reasoning over complex multi-morbidity.
Gap → No agentic behavior — can't retrieve, reconcile, and reason over a full patient history dynamically.
Regulatory writing AI (Regulatory AI, Veeva RIM)
Templates and clause libraries. Can draft sections but can't verify internal consistency across a 50,000-page dossier or flag missing cross-references.
Gap → No agent that autonomously audits a full CTD submission for completeness, consistency, and ICH alignment.
AI call center bots (generic IVR + LLM front-ends)
Can understand intent and answer FAQs. But they don't execute backend scripts to actually resolve issues, don't do skill-matched escalation, and have no concept of GxP compliance audit trails.
Gap → No system combines AI-first resolution + skill-scored human escalation + sentiment handoff + auto-compliant ServiceNow ticket in a pharma context.

Adverse Signal Hunter — multi-source pharmacovigilance agent
Agentic RAG Most novel
🏆 Top pick
An autonomous agent that continuously ingests FDA FAERS, MedDRA-coded case reports, EHR narratives, and PubMed signal literature — triangulating weak signals across sources that no single system sees, detecting rare polypharmacy interactions before they become recalls.
Existing tools see one source at a time. This agent reasons across all of them simultaneously.
Pain point addressed
Pharmacovigilance teams miss rare polypharmacy signals because FDA FAERS, EHR narratives, and literature live in separate systems with no cross-source reasoning.
Why it's unbuilt
Requires real-time multi-source ingestion + semantic search + agent reasoning in a single loop. Previously too compute-expensive. LLM agents make this tractable now.
Hackathon demo
Ingest 500 FAERS cases + 200 papers on a drug pair. Show the agent surfacing a signal that neither source alone reveals, with a full cited evidence trail.
Winning angle
Drug recalls cost $1B+. A system that flags a signal 6 weeks earlier with evidence is a quantifiable, board-level ROI story judges can immediately grasp.
Prototype stack LangGraph agent → RAG over FAERS + PubMed + internal cases (LlamaIndex + Pinecone) → Claude for signal reasoning → alert dashboard with severity scoring + evidence citations
Protocol Deviation Predictor — real-time trial compliance agent
Agentic RAG Most novel
🏆 Top pick
Monitors incoming EDC data streams and site activity logs. Uses RAG over the protocol document + historical deviation data from past trials to predict which sites are 2–3 weeks away from a protocol deviation — and proactively alerts monitors with a specific, explainable risk reason.
Current tools only flag deviations after they happen. This predicts and prevents.
Pain point addressed
Clinical trial monitors discover deviations 2–6 weeks after they occur. By then data is compromised and remediation costs hundreds of thousands of dollars.
Why it's unbuilt
Requires combining protocol semantics (RAG over protocol doc) with live EDC data patterns. Neither standalone analytics nor LLMs alone can do this.
Hackathon demo
Simulate a 10-site trial with synthetic EDC data. Show the agent flagging Site 4 as high-risk 3 weeks before the deviation, citing the exact protocol clause.
Winning angle
Every CRO and sponsor has felt this pain. Predicting before vs. flagging after is a paradigm shift any pharma judge will immediately understand.
Prototype stack LlamaIndex RAG over protocol PDF → LangGraph agent polling synthetic EDC data → anomaly detection layer → proactive alert with natural language explanation + recommended corrective action
Rare Disease Diagnostic Navigator — phenotype-to-diagnosis agent
Agentic RAG
A multi-step reasoning agent that takes a patient's EHR timeline, lab trends, and symptom narrative and runs iterative differential diagnosis — grounded in NORD, Orphanet, and recent case literature. Surfaces the 3 most likely rare conditions with specific evidence citations and next recommended tests.
No tool does longitudinal, multi-modal reasoning grounded in rare disease literature simultaneously.
Pain point addressed
Patients with rare diseases wait 4–8 years for a diagnosis, visiting 7+ specialists. No tool synthesizes their full longitudinal record against rare disease literature.
Why it's unbuilt
Requires multi-step reasoning: symptom extraction → phenotype mapping → differential scoring → literature grounding. No single tool chains all these steps today.
Hackathon demo
Feed a 3-year synthetic patient timeline with 12 symptoms. Show the agent narrowing to 3 rare conditions with specific literature citations and next-step test recommendations.
Winning angle
A 4–8 year patient journey condensed to minutes. Emotionally resonant story + technically impressive demo = presentation judges remember.
Prototype stack LlamaIndex RAG over NORD/Orphanet + PubMed case reports → LangGraph multi-step reasoning agent → EHR timeline parser → ranked differential with evidence citations per condition
MSL Field Intelligence Voice Agent — KOL debrief synthesizer
Voice Agentic
After a KOL meeting, an MSL speaks a 2-minute debrief to a voice agent. The agent structures the insight, classifies it by topic (unmet need, competitive intel, label question), tags it to the relevant product and indication, and routes it to the right team — zero CRM data entry.
MSL insight capture is entirely manual. 60–70% of field intelligence is never recorded.
Pain point addressed
MSLs spend 45 min on CRM entry after every KOL meeting. Field intelligence doesn't reach the right teams in time and most of it evaporates unrecorded.
Why it's unbuilt
Voice + pharma-specific structured extraction + automatic routing hasn't been combined. CRMs are too rigid to capture unstructured medical nuance.
Hackathon demo
Record a 2-min mock MSL debrief live on stage. Show the agent extracting insights, classifying and tagging them, populating a structured intelligence feed in real time.
Winning angle
Live voice demo on stage = instant wow factor. The problem is universally understood by pharma judges who have witnessed the CRM entry burden firsthand.
Prototype stack ElevenLabs / Gemini Live API (voice) → Whisper transcription → Claude extraction agent → LangGraph classification + routing → Supabase persistence → real-time intelligence dashboard
CTD Submission Auditor — regulatory consistency agent
Agentic RAG
Ingests an entire CTD dossier and autonomously checks for cross-module consistency, missing cross-references, and ICH guideline alignment — flagging specific page numbers with plain-language explanations of each issue and a suggested fix.
No tool audits full dossier consistency. Manual QC takes weeks and costs $500K+ per submission.
Pain point addressed
Regulatory teams spend weeks on manual QC of 50,000-page dossiers. A single inconsistency between Module 2 and Module 5 can delay drug approval by months.
Why it's unbuilt
Document-level reasoning across a 50,000-page corpus requires chunked RAG + cross-document agent reasoning — only recently practical with long-context LLMs.
Hackathon demo
Upload 3 CTD modules (real or synthetic). Show the agent finding 5 specific cross-module inconsistencies, citing exact pages, and explaining the regulatory risk of each.
Winning angle
$500K QC cost and months of delay, quantified and preventable. A direct, defensible business case with zero ambiguity about ROI.
Prototype stack LlamaIndex ingestion of CTD modules → cross-module consistency agent (LangGraph) → ICH guideline RAG → structured audit report with issue, page, severity, and suggested fix
Patient Adherence Companion — adaptive conversational agent
Voice RAG
A voice agent that checks in with chronic disease patients daily, understands their actual barriers (cost, side effects, forgetfulness, beliefs) through natural conversation, and adapts its support strategy over weeks — feeding structured adherence signals back to the care team.
Existing adherence tools are notification-based. None reason about why a patient is non-adherent and adapt their approach.
Pain point addressed
50% of chronic disease patients don't adhere to treatment. Providers have no visibility into why. Reminder apps don't understand barriers — they just nag.
Why it's unbuilt
Longitudinal voice relationship + adaptive strategy requires conversational memory + behavioral pattern reasoning — not possible with rule-based chatbots.
Hackathon demo
Simulate 5 days of check-ins with a synthetic patient. Show the agent evolving its approach as it learns the specific barrier (cost), and the care team dashboard reflecting this.
Winning angle
$300B lost annually to medication non-adherence in the US. A system that understands why — and adapts — is a patient-centered story that resonates with any audience.
Prototype stack ElevenLabs voice → LangGraph conversational agent with longitudinal memory (Supabase) → barrier classification layer → care team dashboard with adherence signals and barrier summaries
AURA — Autonomous Unified Resolution Agent for pharma-grade enterprise IT
Voice Agentic Your idea
🏆 Top pick
A self-healing enterprise support agent built for GxP-regulated pharma environments like Gilead. AI-first: resolves issues autonomously via backend sub-agents (password resets, access provisioning, script execution). When it can't resolve, it escalates to the skill-matched human agent — handing off a sentiment analysis, bullet summary, recommended steps, and a pre-filled ServiceNow ticket. Zero documentation burden on the human agent.
Generic AI call center bots don't execute backend scripts, don't do skill-matched escalation, and don't understand GxP compliance audit requirements. AURA does all three.
Pain point addressed
In pharma, a locked-out lab analyst can delay batch release or halt a clinical trial. L1 agents waste time on repetitive resolvable issues, and every manual ticket is an undocumented compliance risk. 3 months of real Gilead call data confirms the pattern.
Why it's genuinely novel
The combination is unbuilt: AI-first resolution with real script execution + skill-scored escalation routing + pre-call sentiment handoff to the human + auto-generated GxP-compliant ServiceNow ticket with work notes. No single tool does all five in a pharma context.
Pharma domain fit
GxP / 21 CFR Part 11 requires every action on regulated systems to be auditable and traceable. AURA's auto-ticket with timestamped steps isn't a convenience — it's a compliance artifact. This reframes "IT support bot" as "regulated environment self-healing agent."
Winning angle
Live demo: Call 1 — AI resolves autonomously (password reset via sub-agent script). Call 2 — AI can't resolve; human agent receives sentiment flag, bullet summary, recommended steps, and a pre-filled ServiceNow ticket before they even say hello. The contrast is the story.
Prototype stack ElevenLabs / Gemini Live (voice) → Whisper STT → LangGraph orchestrator → sub-agents (script executor, AD reset, ServiceNow API) → skill-match escalation router → sentiment analysis layer → human agent handoff dashboard (real-time) → Supabase for session state
Your unfair advantages
✓  3 months of real Gilead L1 call data — you know the top 20 issue types
✓  You understand the actual ServiceNow workflow from the inside
✓  You know which issues have scriptable solutions vs. which need human judgment
✓  Gilead is your demo client — makes the pitch concrete and credible
